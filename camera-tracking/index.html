MachWorks · Virginia Tech · camera tracking · Sep 2025 → Jan 2026

Keeping a camera pointed at a Mach 0.3 jet

MachWorks is Virginia Tech's high-performance aircraft team; its ICARUS program builds and flies a Mach 0.3+ test jet at the Kentland Experimental Aerial Test Range. A fast aircraft is only useful if you can see it, so the team needs a camera that stays on the aircraft for a whole mission without anyone aiming it. I was project manager for the preliminary design of that system and wrote most of the report. It is a design, not a flown system. In January 2026 I moved onto the avionics subteam — that work is on its own page.

What the tracker is for

ICARUS is a fast, small test aircraft, and the team only learns from a flight it can see. The tracker is the ground side of that: it takes a stream of the aircraft's position, works out where the aircraft is and where it will be a moment from now, and slews a gimbal so the camera holds it in frame. It is a pointing-and-estimation problem, and most of the difficulty is in the delay — by the time a position arrives and is processed, the aircraft has already moved.

Designed the tracker — as project manager

The camera-tracking system autonomously points a camera at the aircraft throughout a mission: the ground station (or a small module on the aircraft) streams GPS, the system estimates where the aircraft is and will be, and a gimbal slews the camera to keep it in frame. I led the preliminary design as project manager and wrote most of the design report. The architecture:

  • Pointing. A 3-axis DJI Ronin-S gimbal carrying a Fujifilm X-T2 — chosen over a star-tracker in a weighted trade study for slew speed, range of motion, and cost.
  • Link. A Digi XBee 3 PRO telemetry link (~2 km range, <10 ms latency), chosen over LoRa in a second trade study for latency and integration.
  • Estimation. GPS + IMU + magnetometer fused with a Kalman filter, with trajectory prediction to cover processing and link delay, targeting ±1° pointing.
  • Compute + software. A Raspberry Pi 4B running a ROS 2 node stack: telemetry/sensor interface → target-geometry & control → gimbal command over SBUS → an operator dashboard with rosbag logging.
  • External module. A lightweight ESP32-based tracker that can ride on the subject as a second telemetry source.

The report backed those choices with the trade studies, a requirements-and-verification plan (bench slew tests, laser pointing-accuracy test, field GPS test, range test), a risk matrix, and a component + power budget. It is a Preliminary Design Report — a design, not a flown system — and it was a team deliverable: I was PM of four.
