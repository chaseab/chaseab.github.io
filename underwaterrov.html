Underwater ROV and applicable systems

A tethered underwater vehicle equipped with the systems required to analyze local marine environments: a conductivity, temperature and depth sensor package, a water sampling mechanism, a photogrammetry system, and a telemetry system reporting to the surface over a Cat 6 tether. Constructed for $186.69.

01

Statement of the problem

Oysters filter food through their gills and can remove sediments and other contaminants that pollute the bay. They also consume algae and help to reduce nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus. With the current oyster population, it would require over a year for the entire bay to be filtered.

A number of organizations are working to restore the population to its peak. However, unlike agriculture, aquaculture is rooted in outdated methods of farming with little modern technology in use. Farms and restoration sites using bottom culture have little data on where hard surfaces lie, where sediment would bury young oysters, or which beds have matured. With an affordable underwater ROV carrying several sensors, those operations could gather the data needed to make better decisions regarding planting and harvesting.

In most home-built underwater ROVs, PVC piping is used as a cheap method of creating a frame whilst allowing space to hold wires and onboard electronics. For this project, a port was added to the frame to allow it to flood and achieve a neutral buoyancy. A removable rubber coupling retains one end cap, which allows the internal electronics to be accessible after their initial assembly.

03

Telemetry protocol

Many forms of wireless communication, including radio waves, function poorly through water, so a tethered design was used. Sending data over long cables comes with its own set of challenges. The wires inherently have a small amount of resistance, which subjects the signal to voltage drop, and even small electromagnetic waves can alter the voltage in the wire and distort the signal. With the small voltages used on the Arduino pins, a long run of cabling can significantly impact a signal read against a 2.5 V threshold.

RS-485 was selected for its ability to be used over long distances and resist electrical noise. It uses a twisted pair consisting of an A and a B wire. Instead of reading the voltage of a single wire like UART, RS-485 reads the difference between the two wires. When the signal is altered with electrical noise, the differential voltage remains the same, as the A and B voltages are altered equally. Serial communication on the Arduino is done over UART, so a separate module was required at each end to send and receive the differential voltages.

Single-ended: all 8 bits intact  ·  differential: all 8 bits intact. The differential swings ±5 V against a 0 V threshold, so it retains margin well after the single-ended reading has become unreliable.

In order to communicate more than two values, pulse width modulation is used. Using a set frequency, a cycle consisting of a HIGH and a LOW period is sent, and by reading the percentage of the cycle that is HIGH the Arduino can read a number between 0 and 255. With motor drivers, the same protocol allows an Arduino to vary the voltage supplied to the motors and adjust the speed at which they spin.

The implementation of the RS-485 telemetry modules proved to be very successful. The PlayStation controller, pressure sensor and temperature sensor all functioned well at 9600 baud, allowing the Arduinos to communicate these values roughly 40 times each second.

04

Sensor and water sampling containers

Whilst there is an electronics compartment integrated into the design, the sensors, when interfaced with the water, provided the greatest opportunity for leakage. Both the temperature and pressure sensors required additional ports to access the surrounding environment and, if damaged, could allow water to short any components around them. In order to prevent this possibility, these sensors sit in their own dedicated compartment.

In order to acquire water samples at specified depths, most operations use a Nansen or the improved Niskin bottle, which is lowered into the water open and sealed at depth by a released weight. On the budget allotted, constructing a mechanical device of that kind which interfaced with the Arduino and remained waterproof seemed unlikely. However, the ROV already included a device capable of pumping water. An additional bilge pump was attached to a sealed 4-inch container with a check valve placed in the middle of the excess hosing, and one of the empty buttons on the PlayStation controller was used for activation. The container holds approximately .22 gallons and the pump is rated at 1100 gallons per hour, so powering it for .72 seconds would fill the container completely.

A relay was necessary to ensure the functionality of the bilge pump. When testing the relay with an advertised voltage input range of 4.5–12 volts, it was discovered that the lower end of this range provided insufficient current to activate the relay, instead only triggering the indicator light. Utilizing a 2N2222 transistor in between the 12-volt battery and relay, the Arduino Mega could activate the relay with minimal current.

05

Waterproofing

The main method used to feed wires in and out of the PVC whilst preventing leakage was creating a well of epoxy for the wires to sit in. An ⅛-inch PVC pipe forms the well, and the epoxy cures into a solid plug around the wires passing through it.

However, if bundles of wires were fed through this well and the jacket of the bundle got cut somewhere along the tether, water would have the opportunity to leak in essentially through the wires themselves. In order to prevent leaking of any kind, insulation on the wires was removed where they went through the epoxy.

06

Results

Subsystems were tested individually in water. The assembled vehicle was never operated under power in open water, so no handling, endurance or survey results are reported here.

Telemetry
Successful
The RS-485 modules proved to be very successful. The controller and both sensor channels functioned well at 9600 baud, roughly 40 values each second, without interference.
Sensor compartment
Successful
Both sensors proved to be functional, and the container that housed them did not leak on the first operation underwater.
Data visualization
Successful
Utilizing the Matplotlib library, creating real-time graphs based on the Arduino serial outputs was straightforward. The main obstacle was security policy on the machine preventing Python from accessing certain COM ports.
Pressure → depth
Miscalibrated
The sensor reports, but it is not calibrated correctly, leading to incorrect depth readings. Temperature readings could be used to further hone the depth figures, as pressure is slightly affected by water temperature. This was not implemented.
Water sampling
Undetermined
The mechanism proved to be effective, but its applicability is yet to be determined. The container did not fill completely, as the end caps provided additional, unaccounted-for volume. The greater concern is that the bilge pump was unintentionally filtering the sample as it collected it.
Photogrammetry
Failed
This part of the project was the biggest failure. The software modelled well on the bench and produced nothing usable underwater.
Full vehicle operation
Not attempted
The assembled vehicle was never operated under power in open water. Thruster response, handling and endurance remain unmeasured.

When initially testing the software with a DSLR camera, the 3D models turned out well with a lot of detail; the stack of books imaged was captured well enough to read the text on the covers. However, when attempting to get photos underwater with the GoPro, the images captured did not hold enough detail for the software to properly model the environment. Whilst the extremely wide angle of the GoPro certainly did not help, the main culprit likely lies in the turbidity of the water. Close to a feature, the software lacked enough common reference points to create a model. This function may be useful in a different environment with lower turbidity, but in the Chesapeake Bay it is rendered effectively useless without higher-quality images.
