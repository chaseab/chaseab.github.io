Undergraduate research · Kennedy Lab, Hume Center · since November 2025 · in progress

Shape-memory-alloy wires as cheap, small linear actuators

Since November 2025 I've been an undergraduate researcher in Dr. Scott Kennedy's Intelligent Materials for Biomimetic Sensors and Actuators Lab at Virginia Tech's Hume Center. The lab works on shape-memory-alloy (SMA) actuated robotic ankle systems; my piece is a test rig for characterizing parallel Flexinol wire arrays. This page describes the idea and the rig. Results are held for the paper.

01 / the idea

Run many thin wires in parallel, and fire them in turns

A shape-memory-alloy wire contracts when it's heated (in practice, when you pass current through it) and relaxes as it cools. That makes it a linear actuator with no motor, no gearbox, and almost no volume — attractive for biomimetic joints where a muscle-like pull is what you want. The catch is speed: a wire can only contract again once it has cooled, and cooling is slow.

The approach I'm testing works around the cooling limit instead of fighting it:

  • Parallel arrays. Several thin Flexinol wires share the load instead of one thick wire, so each cools faster.
  • One continuous zigzag. The wire is routed back and forth as a single length, which keeps tension equal across every segment — while each electrical post along the route stays independently controllable.
  • Sequenced firing. Wire pairs activate in turn: some contract while others are still cooling. Output frequency goes up without any individual wire cycling faster.
  • Readiness from resistance. A wire's electrical resistance changes as it recovers thermally, so monitoring resistance tells the controller which wires are ready to fire — no extra sensors on the actuator.

I designed and built the rig and its instrumentation, and it's at single-channel bring-up: one wire pair driven and measured end to end before scaling out to the full array. The characterization runs, and the numbers, are what the manuscript is for — they'll go here once it's submitted.

What I'm not claiming yet — no actuation force, bandwidth, or efficiency figures are published on this page. The design and the instrumentation exist; the measurements are in progress.
